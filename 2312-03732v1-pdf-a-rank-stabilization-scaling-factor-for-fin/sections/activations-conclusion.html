§B.7 + §5 · Activations & Conclusion
pp. 12–13, 5–6

Theorem 3.2's forward-pass condition is stated in terms of the moments of adapter outputs — so an honest validation should check whether those moments actually diverge under LoRA and stabilise under rsLoRA. Post-adapter, pre-LayerNorm activations were recorded across all adapters and averaged over layers throughout training. Both methods produce moments that are small and qualitatively benign — the mean stays near zero, the second raw moment grows gently and stays well below 0.0003.

Why don't the activations separate the two methods the way gradient norms do? Two structural features conspire to hide the effect. LayerNorm immediately follows each adapter, re-centring and rescaling whatever the adapter produces — a layer that actively suppresses moment growth. Averaging all adapters together then further smooths any rank-dependent signal. The gradient norm escapes both of these averaging operations and is therefore the cleaner diagnostic. One difference does survive: with rsLoRA the high-rank curves (r = 512, 2048) level off considerably later for both moments, suggesting that rank-stabilised adapters continue updating their feature directions deep into training — a plausible mechanism for the perplexity improvements that high rsLoRA ranks unlock.

The one-line patch
Replace γr = α / r with γr = α / √r. That single symbol change stabilises gradients across all ranks, turns rank into a real compute-versus-quality dial, and costs nothing at inference — after fine-tuning, γrBA is folded into W to produce a single matrix, identical in shape and latency to the base model.

A natural extension is AdaLoRA , which dynamically redistributes rank across adapters during training. Because adapters in AdaLoRA are free to reach full rank, the collapse that 1/r induces at large rank is especially harmful there — individual adapters can be assigned high rank precisely at moments when the scaling factor most suppresses their gradients. Replacing AdaLoRA's γr = α/r with α/√r is conjectured to recover that lost capacity, with the largest gains expected when the rank budget is generous.

A subtler consequence concerns how to read the original LoRA result that "very low ranks suffice." That conclusion rested on experiments where rank 64 showed no improvement over rank 4 — but under a 1/r scaling, rank 64 gradients are four times smaller at initialisation. The flatness was a measurement artefact of gradient collapse, not evidence about the intrinsic dimensionality of the fine-tuning manifold . With rsLoRA's corrected scaling, the relationship between rank and fine-tuning performance becomes monotone, and the question of how much intrinsic dimension a given task actually requires is genuinely open again — worth revisiting across diverse tasks and model families.

Takeaway
Change one symbol: r → √r in the denominator of γr. The resulting rsLoRA method keeps gradients stable at any rank, transforms rank from a fixed low-value constant into a true compute/quality trade-off knob, and leaves inference cost unchanged.
