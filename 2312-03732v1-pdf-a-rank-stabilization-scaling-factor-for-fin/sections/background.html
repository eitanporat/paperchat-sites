Fine-tuning a large pretrained model updates every weight — an operation that quickly becomes impractical at the scale of modern LLMs . The key insight behind LoRA , building on earlier adapter work and the empirical observation that fine-tuning operates on a low-intrinsic-dimension manifold , is that the weight update itself is low-rank — so a frozen pretrained matrix can be augmented by a cheap rank-r correction.

After fine-tuning, the product W + γrBA is stored as a single matrix — identical in shape to W — so inference cost is unchanged at any rank. The rank-r constraint keeps training cost proportional to r, while r itself acts as a continuous compute knob. Standard LoRA fixes γr = α/r: dividing by a full power of r is, as §3 will show, the source of gradient collapse at large ranks.

Substituting adapter rank r for width d, and LoRA's scaling factor γr for the weight-magnitude exponent a, yields the exact same instability criterion — and, in §3, the unique correction.

Setup for §3
Both LoRA and AdaLoRA scale adapters by α/r — a choice that implicitly sends adapter outputs to zero as rank grows. The abc-parametrization framework provides the machinery to prove, in the r → ∞ limit, exactly which scaling exponent keeps gradients stable — and the answer is not 1/r.
