§3 · rsLoRA: rank-stabilized adapters
pp. 3–4, Appendix A

What should $\gamma_r$ actually do? Scaling the product $BA$ by a rank-dependent constant affects every gradient that passes through the adapter. Too small a $\gamma_r$ and the adapter vanishes into the noise; too large and the forward activations — and backward gradients — blow up. The only safe zone is one where both directions stay bounded as $r \to \infty$. That requirement pins down $\gamma_r$ to a single asymptotic class.

Definition 3.1 — rank-stabilized adapter
An adapter $\gamma_r BA$ is rank-stabilized if both of the following hold for all $r$:
  1. If the inputs to the adapter have $m$-th moment $\Theta_r(1)$ per entry, the outputs do too.
  2. If the loss gradients at the adapter outputs are $\Theta_r(1)$ per entry, the loss gradients back into the inputs are too.

Condition 1 is a forward-pass requirement: the adapter must not amplify or suppress activations as $r$ grows. Condition 2 is its mirror for backpropagation: gradients flowing into the adapter must be no larger or smaller in order than gradients flowing out. An adapter that satisfies both is transparent to the optimizer — rank becomes a pure capacity dial with no side-effects on learning speed.

Theorem 3.2 states the result precisely: given the standard LoRA initialization ($B = 0$, entries of $A$ iid with variance $\sigma_A$ independent of $r$), in expectation over initialization both rank-stability conditions hold simultaneously if and only if $\gamma_r \in \Theta_r(1/\sqrt{r})$. Any $\gamma_r$ that shrinks faster than $1/\sqrt{r}$ — including LoRA's $\alpha/r$ — collapses the gradient. Any $\gamma_r$ that shrinks slower destabilizes activations. The square-root is the only crossover that absorbs the $r$ that emerges from the random initialization of $A$.

The inductive composition argument makes the per-layer guarantee global. Input data carries no $r$-dependence. Apply Theorem 3.2 at the first adapter layer: its output stays $\Theta_r(1)$, which is exactly the assumption the theorem needs for the next layer. Proceeding forward to the loss, and then backward layer by layer, every adapter passes bounded signals in both directions. A single corrected $\gamma_r$ at each adapter suffices; no global re-tuning of the network is required.

Practical consequence
Because the merged weight $(W + \gamma_r BA)$ is rank-independent at inference time, rank is a training-time resource knob with no deployment cost. rsLoRA turns rank into a true compute-for-quality dial: double the rank, spend more training FLOPs, get a strictly better fine-tuned model.

One subtlety the theorem acknowledges: the guarantee concerns gradient magnitude, not gradient direction. If the quality of features learned consistently depends on $r$ for some other reason, the $\Theta_r(1)$ assumption on gradients might not hold. The claim is that, absent such a rank-coupled feature quality effect, stability is guaranteed — and whether higher ranks genuinely yield better features is precisely what the experiments in §4 test.

Proof sketch (Theorem 3.2)

Let $f(x) = \gamma_r B A x$ and track $B_n, A_n$ through $n$ SGD steps with learning rate $\eta$. For $v_n = \nabla_{f(x_n)} L$, the per-step gradients are

$$\nabla_{B_n} L = \gamma_r v_n x_n^T A_n^T, \qquad \nabla_{A_n} L = \gamma_r B_n^T v_n x_n^T.$$

Since $B_0 = 0$, induction gives

$$B_n = \!\left(-\eta\gamma_r \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} v_k x_k^T + O_r(\gamma_r^2)\right) A_0^T, \qquad A_n = A_0\!\left(1 + O_r(\gamma_r^2)\right).$$

Substituting back into the adapter product:

$$\gamma_r B_n A_n = -\gamma_r^2 \eta \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} v_k x_k^T A_0^T A_0 + O_r(\gamma_r^3)\, A_0^T A_0.$$

Now take expectation over the random initialization of $A_0$. Because entries of $A_0$ are iid with variance $\sigma_A$ independent of $r$,

$$\mathbb{E}_{A_0}\!\left[A_0^T A_0\right] = r\,\sigma_A^2\, I.$$

This single step reveals why $r$ appears: the $r$ outer-product terms in $A_0^T A_0$ sum up, injecting an explicit factor of $r$ into both the forward pass and the backward gradient. The $m$-th moment of the adapter's output on a fresh input $x_n$ is therefore

$$\mathbb{E}_{x, A_0}\!\left[\!\left(\gamma_r B_n A_n x_n\right)^m\right] \in \Theta_r\!\left((\gamma_r^2 r)^m\right).$$

The backward gradient into the adapter input (equation 10 in the paper) is likewise in $\Theta_r(\gamma_r^2 r)$. For both to be $\Theta_r(1)$ — the definition of rank-stability — we need $\gamma_r^2 r = \Theta_r(1)$, i.e.

$$\gamma_r \in \Theta_r\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}\right).$$

If $\gamma_r$ shrinks faster (e.g.\ $1/r$) then $\gamma_r^2 r \to 0$ — gradient collapse. If it shrinks slower (e.g.\ $1/r^{1/4}$) then $\gamma_r^2 r \to \infty$ — activation explosion. The $\sqrt{r}$ denominator is the unique crossover. $\blacksquare$
