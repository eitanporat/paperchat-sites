§4 · Discrete JEPA Tokenization
pp. 3–5

The architecture inherits three components from I-JEPA : a context encoder fθc, a target encoder fθ̄t updated by EMA , and predictors gφ. Discrete-JEPA extends this by introducing two distinct token streams inside each encoder: a small set of semantic tokens zs derived from learnable query tokens zs0, and dense patch tokens zp representing fine-grained spatial detail. Vector quantization acts only on the semantic stream; patch tokens stay continuous throughout and are discarded after training.

§4.1 Architecture. The context encoder receives visible patches xV (randomly masked 40–60%) together with the L learnable tokens zs0, and outputs the pair (zs, zp). The target encoder, identically structured but EMA-updated, processes the full image to produce (z̄s, z̄p) — the ground-truth targets predictors are trained to match. Because the target encoder never receives gradients directly, its representations evolve smoothly, providing stable prediction targets in the manner of MoCo and DINO . The encoder backbone follows ViT-Base with patch size 8.

The same shared codebook and lookup are applied to semantic representations from both encoders (equations 4 in the paper), so the discrete tokens entering the predictors and the targets they are matched against live in the same discrete space. Each image is summarized into 8 discrete tokens for Dancing-Sprites-Pattern and 32 discrete tokens for Blinking-Ball, compared to 64 continuous patch tokens per image in I-JEPA. Patch tokens z̄p remain continuous and serve only as an intermediate training signal — they are not retained in the final tokenized output.

Design Principle
VQ acts on the semantic stream only. Patch tokens stay continuous — they carry fine-grained spatial gradients that would be lossy to quantize, and they are discarded after training. Only the compact discrete semantic tokens survive into inference, giving downstream world models a bounded, enumerable state space that eliminates the error accumulation inherent to continuous autoregressive prediction.
