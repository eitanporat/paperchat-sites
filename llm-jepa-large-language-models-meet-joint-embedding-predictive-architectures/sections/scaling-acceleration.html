§5 · generality & compute
pp. 8–9

Every result in §4 rests on a clean premise: Text and Code are two views of the same thing, so the JEPA pull is geometrically justified. A sceptic asks whether the gains evaporate the moment that clean premise breaks down — on open-domain QA where the answer is a handful of tokens, or on commonsense completion where there is no "code" in sight at all.

Two benchmarks stress-test that premise directly. NQ-Open pairs a natural-language question (Text) with a short answer span (Code) — sequences of very different lengths, far from the balanced regex / SQL duality. HellaSwag goes further: both the context and its correct completion are integral parts of the question, with no near-equivalence between them. Yet LLM-JEPA improves Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct on both.

An additional observation from HellaSwag is worth flagging: accuracy keeps improving as λ scales all the way to 1024, with no plateau in sight. The JEPA regularizer can absorb far more weight than conventional loss-balancing intuitions suggest.

The same question applies to reasoning-distilled models, where the internal representations are already heavily shaped by chain-of-thought and reinforcement signals. Adding an embedding-space objective might conflict with those specialised representations — or it might not. Experiments on GSM8K with Qwen3-1.7B and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B show statistically significant gains in both cases: the regularizer composes with reasoning capability rather than fighting it.

The remaining obstacle to broad adoption is compute. Each training step normally requires two forward passes instead of one — a substantial overhead. Random JEPA-loss dropout addresses this directly: on a fraction α of batches the JEPA term is simply skipped, eliminating those extra forward passes entirely. Per-epoch cost becomes 2 − α times the baseline instead of 2×.

The PFLOPs-matched comparison in Figure 5 of the paper drives the point home: LLM-JEPA with LD=0.5 or LD=0.75 converges to higher accuracy than the baseline at the same compute budget — loss dropout is not just a cost-saving concession, it is an accuracy-improving technique in its own right when λ is co-scaled.

Takeaway
LLM-JEPA generalises beyond natural (text, code) pairs — it lifts accuracy on open QA and reasoning benchmarks — and loss dropout makes it competitive on FLOPs, provided λ · (1 − α) is held roughly constant when tuning the dropout rate.
