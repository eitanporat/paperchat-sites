A single embedding-space term added to next-token loss beats standard fine-tuning on every benchmark tested — four model families, four datasets, four model sizes, and LoRA at every rank. The evaluation protocol is uniform across all experiments: five fixed random seeds {82, 23, 37, 84, 4}, best-of-grid hyperparameters for the baseline first, then a grid search over (k, λ) ∈ {0,1,2,3,4} × {0.5,1,2,4} for LLM-JEPA. Accuracy is exact match throughout — regex exact match for NL-RX , final-answer match for GSM8K , and execution-result match for Spider .

Every family improves. The gain ranges from a modest +0.4 pp on an already strong OLMo-2-1B baseline to a +13 pp jump on Llama-3.2-1B and a +13 pp jump on OpenELM — whose gains are also statistically significant at p < 1e-3. Across all four families — Llama-3.2 , Gemma-2 , OpenELM , and OLMo-2 — no model is hurt.

The cross-dataset sweep on Llama-3.2-1B covers NL-RX-TURK (crowdsourced regex descriptions) , GSM8K (grade-school math word problems) , and Spider (cross-domain text-to-SQL) . Gains are consistent: NL-RX-TURK +8.5 pp, GSM8K +4 pp, Spider +3 pp. Every p-value is below 4e-3.

Qualitative comparisons reveal where NTP goes wrong: it tends to add extra tokens, drop quantifier braces, or omit structural operators. LLM-JEPA eliminates most of those systematic errors.

The gains scale with model size. Evaluated on NL-RX-SYNTH across 1B, 3B, 7B, and 8B parameter models, LLM-JEPA improves at every level — including a dramatic +28 pp leap on Llama-3.1-8B, where the baseline suffers from difficulty terminating regex generation.

LoRA fine-tuning carries the same pattern. At LoRA rank 512 (22.6% trainable parameters), LLM-JEPA already matches full fine-tuning accuracy (72.4 vs 70.4%), while NTP at the same rank still trails full fine-tuning by 7 pp. Gains are statistically significant at every rank tested from 32 through full.

Overfitting resistance
Across 6 training epochs on Llama-3.2-1B, standard NTP peaks then degrades; LLM-JEPA accuracy continues to rise. The JEPA term acts as an implicit regulariser, constraining the Text-to-Code mapping within a narrow embedding subspace and preventing the model from memorising surface patterns that do not generalise.

The benefit is not limited to fine-tuning. Training Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct from randomly initialised weights on NL-RX-SYNTH, LLM-JEPA raises accuracy from 54.4 ± 1.7% to 60.6 ± 1.0% (p = 2.94e-4, λ = 2, k = 3), demonstrating that the embedding-space objective improves representation quality even when generative capabilities must be learned from scratch.

Paraphrase pretraining extends the reach further. Pretraining on cestwc/paraphrase (groups of 5 rephrasings of the same sentence) with the i-th paraphrase predicting the (i+1)-th under the JEPA loss, then fine-tuning on Rotten Tomatoes and Yelp with a standard NTP objective, yields statistically significant improvements on both downstream tasks — even though JEPA is not applied at fine-tuning time. This isolates the gain to the pretraining representations themselves, following the evaluation approach of , , and . Rotten Tomatoes improved from 56.6 ± 1.7% to 57.8 ± 1.3% (p = 7.4e-4); Yelp from 26.5 ± 0.9% to 27.2 ± 0.9% (p = 1.0e-3).

Takeaway
Adding a cosine-similarity JEPA term in embedding space — with no change to inference and only one extra forward pass at training time — beats standard next-token-prediction fine-tuning across every model family, dataset, scale, and training regime tested, while simultaneously resisting overfitting.
