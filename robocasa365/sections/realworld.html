§4.5 · Simulation data helps real robots
pp. 9–10

A simulation benchmark earns its keep only if the policies it trains do something useful off-screen. The test moves onto real hardware: a DROID Panda arm with three cameras, set loose in an actual kitchen and driven by GR00T N1.5.

Four kitchen tasks make up the evaluation, ranging from a single flick of the wrist to a two-item tidy-up. Collecting 140 human demonstrations — 30 apiece for the first three tasks, 50 for the longer-horizon last one — seeds the training data.

The two training recipes differ only in whether simulation is invited in. Real Only trains GR00T N1.5 on the 140 real demonstrations; Sim-and-Real first mid-trains on the 150 highest-performing simulation tasks, then co-fine-tunes on the real demos plus matching simulation data for these four tasks.

To make the sim data transfer, the simulation datasets are re-rendered to match the real camera views — following established sim-and-real alignment practice. Those three re-rendered views are exactly what the policy sees at inference.

After training, each model runs 20 trials per task in the real kitchen. Scored as task success rate, the two recipes separate cleanly.

Adding simulation never hurts and usually helps a lot: the biggest jump is PickPlaceToasterOvenToCounter, which climbs from 70% to a perfect 100%, with PickPlaceCounterToCabinet close behind (52% → 84%).

Averaged across the four tasks, mixing in simulation lifts success from 61.8% to 79.8% — a +18.1-point gain over training on real demonstrations alone. The benchmark pays off twice over: once for evaluating policies in sim, and again as free training signal for the real robot.

Takeaway
Pre-training on the simulation tasks and co-fine-tuning with re-rendered sim data raises real-world success from 61.8% to 79.8% — the same synthetic scenes that grade a policy also make it better on hardware.
