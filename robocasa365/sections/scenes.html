§3.1–3.2 · assets and scenes
pp. 3–4

Generalization needs variety, and variety starts with the props. RoboCasa365 keeps RoboCasa's library of 2,509 objects across 153 categories and folds in 57 new artist-made categories — high-quality 3D assets edited to a strict quality bar — pushing the advertised catalog past 3,200 objects.

The bigger jump is in the things a robot actually operates. RoboCasa shipped 20 interactable fixtures and appliances across just four kinds — sinks, coffee machines, stoves, microwaves.

The 456 instances span twelve categories, adding toasters, toaster ovens, stand mixers, blenders, and electric kettles. Every appliance is now articulated, including fridges, ovens, and dishwashers, which were rigid props before.

With the parts in hand, a kitchen becomes a product of two choices: a layout (the floor plan) times a style (which fixtures, appliances, and textures fill it). That factoring is what turns a handful of hand-built rooms into thousands.

The 10 target kitchens come straight from RoboCasa — ten layouts, each matched to one style — and carry most of the evaluations. The pretraining split is built from scratch: 50 new layouts modeled as digital-cousin replicas of 50 real U.S. homes with active Zillow.com listings, crossed with 50 fresh styles.

Crucially, the pretraining and target styles never overlap: no fixture, appliance, or texture is shared between the two splits, so evaluation always lands on rooms the model was never trained to furnish.

Takeaway
Factoring a kitchen into layout × style turns 50 Zillow floor plans and 50 disjoint styles into 2,500 pretraining scenes — while a held-out set of 10 target kitchens keeps every evaluation out of distribution.
