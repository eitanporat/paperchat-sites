§4.2 · foundation model training
p. 7

The paradigm that has carried robot learning forward is pretrain-then-finetune: soak a policy in a broad mixture, then specialize it on the tasks you actually care about. The question this benchmark can finally answer at scale is how much that pretraining is worth in demonstrations saved.

Pretraining pools human data across 300 tasks (411 h) with MimicGen synthetic data across 60 atomic tasks (1,615 h); the target set is human data across 50 tasks (208 h). Of those 50 targets, 34 also appear in pretraining, while 16 Composite-Unseen tasks are held entirely out.

The model throughout is GR00T N1.5, an open foundation policy, evaluated across all 50 target tasks by average success rate. The three budgets — 50, 150, and 500 demos per task — correspond to 10%, 30%, and 100% of the target data.

Pretraining alone clears 41.9% on atomic tasks but collapses to near zero on both composite splits — broad skills transfer, but the long-horizon compositions that were never demonstrated do not appear from thin air.

Add target post-training and pretraining wins at every budget: 35.9 vs. 21.0 at 10%, 42.2 vs. 34.3 at 30%, 51.1 vs. 43.7 at 100%. The gap is widest on Composite-Unseen, where fine-tuning a pretrained model reaches 42.1 against 33.3 from scratch.

Read the curves horizontally and the payoff is a ~3× gain in data efficiency: the pretrained-and-finetuned policy matches target-only quality using roughly one-third the target demonstrations. Its 50-demo point (35.9) already sits above target-only's 150-demo point (34.3).

The central result
Pretraining buys about a 3× data-efficiency multiplier — it reaches the same downstream success as learning from scratch with roughly one-third the target demonstrations, and the gain is largest exactly where it is hardest to earn: the compositions never seen during pretraining.
