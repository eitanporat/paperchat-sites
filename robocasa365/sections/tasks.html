§3.3 · the tasks
p. 4

A kitchen agent has to do many small things well and a few long things in order. RoboCasa365 splits that ambition into two kinds of task: atomic tasks, which execute a single skill, and composite tasks, which chain skills into a routine — 365 tasks in all, 65 atomic and 300 composite.

The atomic tasks rest on eight foundational skills inherited from RoboCasa. RoboCasa contributed 25 atomic tasks; 40 new ones cover appliances and behaviors the simulator now affords, bringing the atomic set to 65.

Composite tasks are where the kitchen comes alive — brewing coffee, plating a dish, loading the dishwasher — and there are far too many to author by hand. So the 300 composite tasks are authored by a two-stage LLM pipeline: first solicit activities, then expand each into concrete task blueprints, then write code.

Those 300 tasks don't sit in a flat list. They organize into 6 activity families spanning 60 activities, grouped by shared functional and semantic structure — a beverage task and a cooking task ask for different things even when both start with a pick-and-place.

Under each family sit its activities, and under those the individual tasks. Cooking alone branches into baking, boiling, frying, making toast, and sautéing; serving covers setting the table, plating, and garnishing.

One cut runs across both task types and matters most for hardware: 220 of the 365 tasks require mobile manipulation — the robot must drive between fixtures — while 145 can be finished from a single spot.

Takeaway
365 everyday tasks fall out of 8 reusable skills: 65 atomic tasks exercise them one at a time, and 300 LLM-authored composite tasks chain them into the six families of a working kitchen — most demanding that the robot move to get the job done.
