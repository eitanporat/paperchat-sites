§1 · The case for a simulation benchmark
pp. 1–2

Robot learning has edged close to generalist policies — single models that manipulate novel objects across unfamiliar rooms. Two obstacles keep that promise from scaling.

Simulation answers both at once: it can synthesize an effectively infinite variety of tasks and scenes, and it turns evaluation into a controlled, reproducible procedure. The catch is that existing frameworks spend little of that budget — a handful of tasks, a single tabletop or room, and datasets too small to probe how diversity and scale actually drive generalization.

RoboCasa365, built on the RoboCasa framework, spends the full budget — a household-robotics benchmark organized around four pillars.

Synthetic hours come from MimicGen, which expands a few human demonstrations into many by replaying them across scene variations. Together the four pillars form a single resource for training policies and asking what makes them generalize.

The scale gap is stark: where the prior RoboCasa release spanned 30 tasks and 100 scenes with 100k demonstrations, RoboCasa365 spans 300+ tasks and 2,500 scenes with over 500k demonstrations — the first simulation framework to combine that breadth of tasks, scenes, data, and benchmarks at once.

Takeaway
Diverse data and reproducible evaluation are the twin bottlenecks for generalist robots — and a simulator big enough to hold 365 tasks, 2,500 real-home kitchens, and 2,000+ hours of experience is how you attack both with one instrument.
