§3.4 · datasets
pp. 5–6

Behind the benchmark sits a robot-data collection that clears 2,000 hours and half a million trajectories — enough to train policies on everyday household work at a scale most simulated manipulation datasets never reach.

Every demonstration comes from a Franka Panda Emika arm mounted on an Omron mobile base; the simulation framework can in principle drive other mobile manipulators and humanoid platforms.

The synthetic slice is by far the largest. For each of 60 atomic tasks, MimicGen takes the 100 human teleop demonstrations as seeds and stamps out 10k demos — a 100× scale-up, contributing 1,615 hours on its own.

The human demonstrations still anchor the collection: 30k pretraining demos across 300 tasks, plus 500 demos apiece for the 50 target tasks. Two distributions describe how those episodes are shaped.

Most of the 365 tasks decompose into just one or two subtasks, but a long tail runs all the way out to 15 and 16 subtasks — the long-horizon chores that make the benchmark hard.

Across all 55k pretraining and target human episodes, most last between 10 and 60 seconds, with a tail of longer-horizon runs that stretch past three minutes. (Bucketed counts read off the published histogram — approximate.)

Takeaway
The distributions share one shape: a dense mass of short, simple tasks and a thin tail of long, multi-subtask ones. That tail — the 15-subtask chores and the three-minute episodes — is exactly the long-horizon behavior that a household robot has to get right.
